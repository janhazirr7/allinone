How to Use the OmniTools Date Calculator

Dates matter for almost everything in daily life: a holiday you are counting down to, a project deadline, a visa expiry date, a subscription renewal, or a contract notice period. Doing that counting by hand is slow and easy to get wrong, because months have different lengths and leap years add an extra day. This calculator does the counting for you, in seconds, with two simple modes.

  1. Choose your mode Pick "Difference between two dates" to measure the gap between two calendar dates, or "Add or subtract days from a date" to jump forward or backward in time from a single starting date.
  2. Enter your dates For a difference, enter a start date and an end date. The end date must be on or after the start date; if it is earlier, the calculator will show a friendly error instead of a confusing negative number. For adding or subtracting, enter your base date, a whole number of days, and whether to add or subtract.
  3. Click "Calculate" You will instantly see the total days, the same gap expressed in weeks, a calendar-accurate years, months, and days breakdown, and an approximate total in months. In add or subtract mode, you will see the resulting date, written out in full, together with its day of the week.
  4. Copy or reset Use "Copy Summary" to copy a plain-text summary you can paste into a message or document, or press "Reset" to clear the form and start a new calculation.

How the Date Difference Is Counted

This calculator defines the difference as the end date minus the start date, measured in whole calendar days. In plain words: the start date itself is not counted, and the end date is counted. So from Monday to Tuesday is 1 day, from 1 January to 31 January is 30 days, and two identical dates are 0 days apart. This is the same convention used by most countdown tools and booking systems, and it is the only convention that makes the maths consistent: if you add the difference back to the start date, you land exactly on the end date.

Some people need the inclusive count instead, where both the first and the last day are counted — for example, a hotel stay from Monday to Friday might be described as five days if both days are included in the booking. That is simply the difference plus one, and the calculator shows it for you in the results note, so you never have to guess which number someone else meant. Always check which convention a contract, a booking, or a deadline uses, because a single day can change a price or a penalty.

The calculator counts in whole days only, ignoring hours, minutes, and time zones. That is deliberate: a date on its own has no time of day, and counting by calendar dates avoids the off-by-one errors that appear when a computer converts a date into a local midnight that shifts with daylight saving time. Behind the scenes, every date is treated as midnight in Coordinated Universal Time, so the same two dates always give the same answer no matter where in the world you are.

Years, Months, Days Breakdown and Leap Years

A raw number of days is useful, but most people think in years and months: "How long until my anniversary?" or "How old is this contract?" The breakdown shown by this calculator works like an age calculation. It first counts whole calendar years from the start date, then whole calendar months, and finally the remaining days. When the end day of the month is smaller than the start day, it borrows days from the previous month, exactly as you would when working it out on paper. The result always adds back up to the end date, even across months of different lengths.

Leap years are the classic trap in date maths. A year is a leap year if it is divisible by 4, except for century years, which must also be divisible by 400. So 2024 was a leap year, 2023 was not, 2000 was a leap year, but 1900 was not. In a leap year, February has 29 days instead of 28, which changes every difference that crosses late February. For example, 28 February 2024 to 1 March 2024 is 2 days, because 29 February sits in between, while the same pair of dates in 2023 is only 1 day. This calculator handles leap years automatically, including the rare century rule, so you never need to check a calendar yourself.

The "total months (approx.)" figure is different from the breakdown: it simply divides the total days by 30.44, the average length of a month over a four-year cycle including one leap year. It is handy for rough planning, such as estimating how many monthly payments fall inside a period, but for exact dates you should always use the years, months, and days breakdown or the add and subtract mode, which land on a precise calendar date.

A Note on Business Days, Plus Everyday Examples

This calculator counts calendar days, including Saturdays and Sundays, and it does not remove public holidays. Holidays differ from country to country, and even from region to region, so no single holiday list would be correct for every visitor. If you need working days only, a practical shortcut is to take the total calendar days, divide by seven to estimate the number of full weeks, and subtract two days per full week for the weekend, then adjust for any holidays inside the period. For legal or employment deadlines, always confirm the exact rule with the organisation involved, because some count the first day and others do not.

Whatever you are counting, the safest habit is to calculate it twice: once as a difference and once by adding the result back to the start date. If the second calculation lands on your end date, your answer is confirmed. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

How is the difference between two dates counted?
The difference is end date minus start date, in whole calendar days. The start date itself is not counted, and the end date is counted. For example, from 1 January to 2 January is 1 day. If you want to count both the start and the end date, add 1 to the result — that inclusive count is also shown in the results.
Why is 28 February 2024 to 1 March 2024 equal to 2 days?
Because 2024 is a leap year, February has 29 days. Counting forward from 28 February, the next day is 29 February (day 1) and the day after that is 1 March (day 2). In a non-leap year, the same pair of dates would be only 1 day apart, because there is no 29 February.
What does the years, months, and days breakdown mean?
It is a calendar-accurate breakdown, like an age calculation. Whole years and whole calendar months are counted first, and the remaining days are what is left over. Because months have different lengths (28 to 31 days), the breakdown borrows days from the previous month when needed, so it always lands exactly on the end date.
Does this calculator count business days?
No. This calculator counts calendar days, including Saturdays and Sundays. It does not remove weekends or public holidays, because holidays differ by country and region. If you need working days only, take the total days and subtract the weekends that fall inside the period.
Is my date information saved or sent anywhere?
Never. OmniTools works entirely in your own web browser. The dates you enter are never sent to a server, stored in a database, or shared with anyone.
