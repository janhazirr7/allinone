How to Use the Tip Calculator

Splitting a restaurant bill should take seconds, not a phone passed around the table while everyone argues about the math. This calculator works out the tip, the total, and exactly what each person owes, including the tip share per person, so nobody overpays and nobody is left covering the difference. It runs entirely in your browser, with no account and no data stored anywhere.

  1. Enter the bill amountType the total before tip, for example 100 for a hundred-dollar meal.
  2. Choose a tip percentageTap 10%, 15%, 18%, 20%, or 25%, or type any percentage from 0 to 100 if you want something different.
  3. Enter the number of peopleType how many people are splitting the bill, from 1 to 100. Use 1 if you are paying alone and just want the tipped total.
  4. CalculatePress Calculate Tip to see the tip amount, the total to pay, what each person pays, and the tip included in each share.

The Tip Formulas Explained

There are only three steps, and each one follows directly from the last. Once you see them written out, you can check any tip calculation on paper or in your head, and you will immediately spot the most common mistake: tipping on the total after tax instead of the pre-tax bill, which quietly inflates the tip.

Tip = Bill × (Tip % / 100)
Where:
• Total to pay = Bill + Tip
• Each person pays = Total to pay / Number of people
• Tip per person = Tip / Number of people

Rounding is worth a moment of thought when splitting. If a total of $115 is split four ways, each share is $28.75 exactly, but many totals do not divide neatly. In practice, groups usually round each share up to the nearest dollar or let one person cover the odd cents. The calculator shows exact cents so the group can decide fairly, rather than hiding a rounding gap that someone has to absorb.

Worked Example: $100 Bill, 15% Tip, 4 People

A group of four finishes a meal with a $100 bill and agrees on a 15% tip. The tip is $100 × 15 / 100, which equals $15. The total to pay is $115, each person pays $115 / 4, which is $28.75, and the tip included in each share is $15 / 4, which is $3.75. Everyone can see the same numbers, which ends the usual debate about who owes what and whether the tip was included.

Tipping customs vary a lot by country, and the percentage you choose should follow local norms and the quality of service. In the United States, 15% to 20% is standard for table service, with 18% to 20% common in cities. In much of Europe, a small tip or rounding up is normal because a service charge is often already included, so always check the bill for a service line before adding a tip on top of it. In Japan, tipping is generally not expected at all. When in doubt, a polite question to the server avoids both undertipping and double-tipping.

1. Check for a service charge first

If the bill already includes a service charge, usually 10% to 15%, that amount is the tip in many countries. Adding another full tip on top means paying twice for the same service.

2. Tip on the pre-tax amount

The usual convention is to calculate the tip on the food and drink subtotal, before sales tax. Tipping on the taxed total raises the tip by the tax rate for no extra service.

3. Splitting uneven orders fairly

This calculator splits equally, which suits groups that shared food. If one person ordered far more than the rest, calculate their share separately or let them pay their own items plus their tip.

A good tip rewards good service and keeps the evening pleasant for everyone, including the staff. Using a calculator removes the awkward arithmetic from the moment, so the group can settle quickly, thank the server properly, and move on to the rest of the night.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

How much should I tip at a restaurant?
It depends on the country and the service. In the United States, 15% to 20% of the pre-tax bill is standard for table service. In much of Europe, rounding up or 5% to 10% is common when no service charge is included. Always check the bill for a service charge first.
Should I tip on the pre-tax or post-tax amount?
The usual convention is the pre-tax subtotal: food and drink before sales tax is added. Tipping on the post-tax total raises the tip by the tax rate. This calculator uses the bill amount you enter, so enter the pre-tax subtotal if you want to follow that convention.
How do I split a bill fairly between friends?
For shared meals, divide the total including tip equally: each person pays (bill + tip) / number of people. For very uneven orders, each person should pay for their own items plus tip on those items, and shared items should be split equally.
What if the number of people is 1?
Enter 1 and the calculator simply shows the tip and the total for one payer. The per-person amount then equals the full total, which is useful when you just want to know the tipped total for yourself.
Is my bill amount saved or sent anywhere?
No. OmniTools runs entirely in your browser. Your bill, tip percentage, and number of people are never sent to a server, stored in a database, or shared with anyone.
