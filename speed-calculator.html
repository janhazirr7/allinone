How to Use the Speed Calculator

Speed, distance, and time are tied together by one simple relationship: if you know any two of them, you can always work out the third. Drivers use it to estimate arrival times, runners use it to plan race paces, and students meet it in almost every physics course. This calculator does all three rearrangements for you, and it converts between kilometres per hour, miles per hour, and metres per second, so you can mix the units you actually have instead of converting them by hand first.

  1. Fill in exactly two boxesEnter any two of distance, time, and speed, and leave the third box empty. The empty box is the value the calculator will find for you.
  2. Choose the unit for each valueDistance can be in kilometres, miles, or metres; time in hours, minutes, or seconds; speed in km/h, mph, or m/s. The answer is given in the unit you selected for the missing value.
  3. Press CalculateThe missing value appears instantly. If you filled in all three boxes, or only one, the calculator asks you to correct that instead of guessing.
  4. Check that the answer makes senseA car trip of 120 km at 60 km/h should take 2 hours. If your answer looks wildly different, check whether a unit is wrong, for example minutes entered as hours.

The Three Formulas: One Relationship, Three Arrangements

The core formula is speed equals distance divided by time. Rearranging it gives the other two forms: distance equals speed multiplied by time, and time equals distance divided by speed. These are not three separate facts to memorize; they are the same triangle of quantities with a different corner covered up. If you remember that speed sits on top of distance over time, you can recover any of the three forms by covering the unknown and reading what remains. That is exactly how this calculator works internally: it converts everything to metres and seconds, applies the right arrangement, and converts the answer back to your chosen unit.

speed = distance / time   |   distance = speed × time   |   time = distance / speed
Worked example: a train covers 240 km in 3 hours. Its speed is 240 / 3 = 80 km/h. Driving 80 km/h for 2.5 hours covers 80 × 2.5 = 200 km. Covering 200 km at 80 km/h takes 200 / 80 = 2.5 hours.

Unit conversion is where most mistakes happen, and it is worth learning the key factors. One mile is exactly 1609.344 metres, so 60 mph is about 96.56 km/h, not 60 km/h. One metre per second equals 3.6 kilometres per hour, because there are 3600 seconds in an hour and 1000 metres in a kilometre, so 10 m/s is 36 km/h. Time conversions are equally easy to slip on: 90 minutes is 1.5 hours, not 1.9 hours, and 45 minutes is 0.75 hours. This calculator handles all of these conversions for you, but knowing the factors helps you spot an answer that cannot be right.

Everyday Uses: Trips, Running, and Reaction Distances

Road trips are the most common use. If a journey is 300 km and you average 100 km/h on the motorway, the driving time is 3 hours, before stops, traffic, and fuel breaks, which is why real arrival estimates always add a margin. Runners think in pace rather than speed: pace is time per unit distance, the inverse of speed. A runner covering 10 km in 50 minutes averages 12 km/h, which is a pace of 5 minutes per kilometre. Cyclists, swimmers, and rowers use the same relationship with their own units, and this calculator works for all of them as long as the motion is at a roughly steady speed.

One important limitation deserves emphasis: these formulas assume constant speed. Real journeys involve accelerating, braking, traffic lights, and hills, so the speed you calculate is always an average over the whole trip, not the speed shown on the dashboard at any moment. If a car covers 120 km in 2 hours, its average speed is 60 km/h even if it spent half the trip at 90 km/h and half stuck in traffic at 30 km/h. For safety calculations such as stopping distance, average speed is not enough, because braking distance grows with the square of speed; doubling speed roughly quadruples braking distance. Keep that in mind whenever a calculation touches real driving decisions, and remember that this tool, like all of OmniTools, runs entirely in your browser without sending your numbers anywhere.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

How do I calculate speed from distance and time?
Divide distance by time. For example, 240 km in 3 hours is 240 / 3 = 80 km/h. Enter the distance and time, leave speed blank, and this calculator does the division and any unit conversion.
How do I convert mph to km/h?
Multiply by 1.609344. So 60 mph is 60 x 1.609344 = 96.56 km/h. This calculator applies the exact factor when your speed unit differs from your distance and time units.
What if I enter all three values?
The calculator asks you to enter exactly two values and leave the third blank, because the blank one is the answer. Entering all three would leave nothing to solve for.
Why is my trip time estimate different from the calculator?
The calculator assumes constant speed, so its time is a pure driving time. Real trips add stops, traffic, and slower sections, so add a margin to the calculated time for a realistic arrival estimate.
What is the difference between speed and pace?
Speed is distance per unit of time, such as km/h. Pace is the inverse: time per unit of distance, such as minutes per kilometre. A runner at 12 km/h has a pace of 5 minutes per kilometre.
