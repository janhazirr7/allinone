How to Use the VAT Calculator

Value Added Tax, usually shortened to VAT, is the consumption tax added to most goods and services in Europe and many other countries. Prices on shop shelves usually already include VAT, while business-to-business quotes often exclude it, and that mismatch causes constant confusion. This calculator handles both directions: add VAT to a net price to get the gross price a customer pays, or strip VAT out of a gross price to find the net price and the tax hidden inside it.

  1. Choose the calculation modePick Add VAT if you have a net price and want the gross price, or Remove VAT if you have a gross price and want to extract the net price and tax.
  2. Enter the priceType the price you already have: the net price in Add mode, or the gross price in Remove mode.
  3. Enter the VAT rateType the rate as a percentage, for example 20 for the standard UK rate, 21 for Spain, or 19 for Germany. Reduced rates apply to some goods, so check the correct rate for your product.
  4. CalculatePress Calculate VAT to see the net price, the VAT amount, and the gross price side by side.

The VAT Formulas Explained, Both Directions

Adding VAT is simple multiplication, but removing VAT trips up many people, because the tax is a percentage of the net price, not of the gross price. Removing 20% from a gross price does not remove the VAT: it removes too much, because 20% of the gross price is larger than 20% of the smaller net price. The correct reverse calculation divides by one plus the rate.

Adding VAT: Gross = Net × (1 + rate/100)
Removing VAT: Net = Gross / (1 + rate/100)
• VAT amount = Gross − Net (in Remove mode) or Net × rate/100 (in Add mode)
• Example at 20%: a $100 net price becomes $120 gross, and a $120 gross price divides back to exactly $100 net, with $20 of VAT.

This distinction matters for freelancers and small businesses reclaiming VAT, for shoppers checking whether a discount was applied before or after tax, and for anyone comparing prices across countries with different rates. Always confirm whether a quoted price is net or gross before signing a contract or issuing an invoice, because a 20% misunderstanding changes the total by a full fifth of the net price.

Worked Examples: Adding and Removing 20% VAT

Adding VAT: a product priced at $100 net with a 20% VAT rate carries $20 of tax, since $100 × 20 / 100 equals $20, so the gross price the customer pays is $120. Removing VAT: a product priced at $120 gross with the same 20% rate has a net price of $120 / 1.20, which equals $100, and the VAT inside it is $120 − $100, which is $20. Both directions agree, as they must, because they describe the same transaction from opposite sides.

The common mistake is to calculate 20% of $120, which is $24, and subtract it to get $96. That answer is wrong by $4, because the VAT was never 20% of the gross price: it was 20% of the $100 net price. Whenever a reverse VAT result looks slightly off, check whether the percentage was taken from the gross figure by mistake, since that is the error almost every time.

1. Freelancers and invoices

If you quote $1,000 net and your VAT rate is 20%, invoice $1,200 gross. If a client gives you a $1,200 gross budget instead, your net revenue is $1,000, not $960, so price your work from the net figure.

2. Comparing prices across borders

The same product can look cheaper abroad simply because of a lower VAT rate. Strip VAT out of both prices to compare the true net cost before deciding where to buy.

3. Reduced and zero rates

Food, books, medicine, and children's goods often carry reduced or zero rates that differ by country. Use the rate for your specific product category, not the standard rate, when the category qualifies.

VAT rules, rates, and exemptions change over time and differ by country, so this calculator gives you the arithmetic, not tax advice. For business filings, confirm the current rate and the correct treatment with your local tax authority or accountant.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is the difference between a net price and a gross price?
The net price excludes VAT, while the gross price includes it. A $100 net price at 20% VAT becomes a $120 gross price. Business quotes are often net, while shop prices shown to consumers are usually gross.
Why can't I just subtract 20% from a gross price to remove VAT?
Because VAT is a percentage of the net price, not the gross price. On a $120 gross price at 20% VAT, the tax is $20, not $24. The correct reverse formula is net = gross / 1.20, which gives $100, not $96.
What VAT rate should I use?
Use the standard rate of the country where the sale happens, unless the product qualifies for a reduced or zero rate. Common standard rates include 20% in the UK, 21% in Spain, and 19% in Germany, but rates change, so check the current rate for your product and country.
Does this calculator handle reduced VAT rates?
Yes. Enter any rate from 0% to 100%, including reduced rates such as 5% or 10% and a 0% zero rate. The same add and remove formulas apply at every rate.
Is my price data saved or sent anywhere?
No. OmniTools runs entirely in your browser. Your price and VAT rate are never sent to a server, stored in a database, or shared with anyone.
