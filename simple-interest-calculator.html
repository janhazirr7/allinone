How to Use the Simple Interest Calculator

Simple interest is the most straightforward way interest is ever calculated: a fixed percentage of the original amount, multiplied by the number of years. It is the method used for many short-term loans, some car loans, and basic savings products, and it is also the easiest interest calculation to check by hand. This calculator does it instantly and shows the interest per year as well, so you can see exactly what each year costs or earns.

  1. Enter the principalType the original amount borrowed or invested, for example 10000.
  2. Enter the annual rateType the yearly percentage, for example 5 for five percent. The rate stays fixed for the whole period in a simple-interest calculation.
  3. Enter the time in yearsType the duration, for example 3 for three years. Half-year values such as 2.5 are accepted.
  4. CalculatePress Calculate Interest to see the interest amount, the total repayable or receivable amount, and the interest charged or earned per year.

The Simple Interest Formula Explained

Unlike compound interest, simple interest never earns interest on itself. Every year costs exactly the same amount, because the calculation always returns to the original principal. That predictability is its main advantage: borrowers know from day one what every year will cost, and there are no surprises as the balance changes.

Interest = (P × R × T) / 100
Where:
• P = principal, the original amount
• R = annual interest rate in percent
• T = time in years
• Total amount = P + Interest
• Interest per year = Interest / T

The formula is linear, which means doubling any one input doubles the interest: twice the principal, twice the rate, or twice the time all produce twice the interest. That simple proportionality makes it easy to compare offers quickly. If one lender offers 6% for 4 years and another offers 8% for 3 years on the same principal, the first costs 24% of the principal in interest while the second costs 24% as well, so on interest alone they are identical.

Worked Example: $10,000 at 5% for 3 Years

Borrow $10,000 at a simple annual rate of 5% for 3 years. The interest is ($10,000 × 5 × 3) / 100, which equals $1,500. The total amount to repay is therefore $11,500, and the interest per year is $500. There is nothing hidden in that result: year one costs $500, year two costs $500, and year three costs $500, because the interest is always calculated on the original $10,000 rather than on a growing balance.

Now compare the same inputs under compound interest, where the total would be $11,576.25 with yearly compounding. The difference of $76.25 is small over three years, but it grows quickly over longer periods, which is exactly why lenders and savers must always check which method a product uses. A loan advertised with a low rate can still be expensive if it compounds monthly, while a simple-interest loan with a slightly higher rate can be cheaper overall.

1. Short-term loans

Simple interest is common for short personal loans and some auto financing, where the lender wants a predictable, easy-to-explain repayment schedule without monthly compounding.

2. Checking a quote

If a lender quotes total interest that is far above (principal × rate × years) / 100, the product is probably compounding or includes fees. Ask which method is used before signing anything.

3. Early repayment

With simple interest, repaying early usually saves a clean, proportional amount of interest, because each remaining year would have cost the same fixed sum. Confirm there is no early-repayment penalty first.

Remember that real loans often add fees, insurance, or taxes on top of interest, so the calculator result shows the pure interest cost. Use it to compare the core cost of different offers, then add any fees a lender discloses to get the true total.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is simple interest?
Simple interest is interest calculated only on the original principal amount. It stays the same every year because it never includes interest earned in earlier years, which makes it easy to predict and verify.
What is the simple interest formula?
Interest equals principal times rate times time, divided by 100: I = (P × R × T) / 100. The total amount is the principal plus that interest. For example, $10,000 at 5% for 3 years gives $1,500 of interest and a total of $11,500.
Is simple interest better than compound interest?
It depends on your side of the deal. For a borrower, simple interest is usually cheaper, because interest does not compound. For a saver or investor, compound interest is better, because earnings are reinvested and grow faster over time.
Can time be a fraction of a year?
Yes. Time can be entered as a decimal, such as 2.5 years, and the formula scales proportionally. Six months at 5% on $10,000 gives $250 of simple interest, exactly half of a full year.
Is my financial data saved or sent anywhere?
No. OmniTools runs entirely in your browser. Your principal, rate, and time are never sent to a server, stored in a database, or shared with anyone.
