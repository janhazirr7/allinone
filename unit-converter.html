How to Use the OmniTools Unit Converter

Whether you are following a recipe from another country, checking a road distance on holiday, reading a weather forecast, or buying fabric online, you will sooner or later need to change a measurement from one unit to another. The OmniTools Unit Converter does that work for you in one place, with no sign-up, no app install, and no data leaving your device. Everything is calculated instantly in your browser, so you can convert as many values as you like, as fast as you can type them.

  1. Choose a category Pick Length, Weight or Temperature. The unit lists below change automatically, so you only ever see units that belong together.
  2. Enter your value Type the number you want to convert, for example 5 for five kilometers, or 72 for seventy-two degrees Fahrenheit.
  3. Pick the From and To units Choose the unit you have and the unit you want. Use the Swap button if you want to flip the direction, for example from miles to kilometers instead of kilometers to miles.
  4. Click Convert You will see the headline result, the formula that was used, the value in the base unit, the reverse conversion, and a table showing your value in every unit of that category — handy when you are comparing several units at once.

A useful tip: converting from a unit to itself is perfectly fine and simply returns the same value. That can be a quick sanity check — if 10 meters converts to 10 meters, the tool is working exactly as expected, and you can then change just the target unit.

Length and Weight: One Simple Rule

Length and weight are the easy cases, because every unit in the same family measures from the same zero. That means each conversion is a single multiplication or division. The converter first changes your value into a base unit — meters for length, kilograms for weight — and then changes that base value into your target unit. Because the factors are exact by definition, the results are exact too, apart from the small rounding used only to keep the display readable.

Result = Value × (From-unit in base units) ÷ (To-unit in base units)
Example: 5 km to miles = 5 × 1000 ÷ 1609.344 = 3.10686 miles.
Example: 1 kg to pounds = 1 × 1 ÷ 0.45359237 = 2.20462 pounds.

Two conversions come up again and again in everyday life. A mile is 1.609344 kilometers, so a 10-kilometer run is about 6.21 miles, and a marathon at 26.2 miles is about 42.2 kilometers. For weight, a pound is just under half a kilogram, so 150 pounds is about 68 kilograms, and a 5-kilogram bag of flour weighs about 11 pounds. Keeping those two anchor points in mind makes it easy to sense-check any result at a glance.

Temperature: Different Zeros, Real Formulas

Temperature is different from length and weight, and that difference trips people up. Celsius and Fahrenheit do not share the same zero: water freezes at 0 degrees Celsius but at 32 degrees Fahrenheit, and it boils at 100 degrees Celsius but at 212 degrees Fahrenheit. Because the zero points differ, you cannot convert temperature with a single multiplication. Every conversion needs a multiplication and an addition or subtraction, and this converter uses the real formulas rather than an approximation.

°F = °C × 9/5 + 32   |   °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9   |   K = °C + 273.15
Full set used by this converter:
• Celsius to Fahrenheit: °F = °C × 9/5 + 32
• Fahrenheit to Celsius: °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9
• Celsius to Kelvin: K = °C + 273.15
• Kelvin to Celsius: °C = K − 273.15
• Fahrenheit to Kelvin: K = (°F − 32) × 5/9 + 273.15
• Kelvin to Fahrenheit: °F = (K − 273.15) × 9/5 + 32

Everyday anchors: 0 °C = 32 °F (freezing), 20 °C = 68 °F (a pleasant room), 37 °C = 98.6 °F (body temperature), 100 °C = 212 °F (boiling).

Kelvin deserves a special note. It is the scientific temperature scale, and it starts at absolute zero — the coldest temperature that can physically exist. Absolute zero is 0 kelvin, which equals minus 273.15 degrees Celsius. That is why this converter rejects any Kelvin value below zero: such a temperature cannot exist, and showing a converted number for it would be misleading. Negative Celsius and Fahrenheit values, by contrast, are completely normal and are accepted without any problem.

Metric vs. Imperial: Why Both Still Matter

Most of the world uses the metric system, built on clean powers of ten: 100 centimeters in a meter, 1000 meters in a kilometer, 1000 grams in a kilogram. It is easy to learn and easy to scale up or down. The imperial system — inches, feet, miles, pounds and ounces — is still the everyday system in the United States and appears in many older recipes, product labels, sports measurements and road signs around the world. That split is exactly why a converter is useful: a recipe in cups and ounces, a height given in feet and inches, or a car speed limit in miles per hour all need translating when you live, cook or travel across systems.

1. Cooking across countries

A recipe that calls for 500 grams of flour is asking for about 17.6 ounces, or just over 1.1 pounds. Converting before you start avoids guessing and keeps the recipe working as the author intended.

2. Travel and road distances

A sign saying the next town is 30 miles away means about 48 kilometers. Converting distances before a trip helps you judge fuel, time and rest stops in the units you think in.

3. Weather in another country

A forecast of 77 degrees Fahrenheit sounds hot until you convert it: it is 25 degrees Celsius, a warm but ordinary summer day. Temperature conversion turns an unfamiliar number into weather you can actually picture.

One final caution applies to every converter, not just this one: be careful with rounded results when precision matters. For everyday use, a result rounded to a few decimal places is more than enough. For engineering, medicine, laboratory work or legal measurements, always double-check against the official definition of the unit and keep full precision through your calculations, rounding only at the very end. This converter shows enough digits for everyday decisions while keeping the headline result easy to read, and its underlying factors are the exact definitions, so the math underneath is as accurate as the units themselves.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

How many feet are in one meter?
One meter equals exactly 3.28084 feet, because one foot is defined as exactly 0.3048 meters. To convert meters to feet, divide by 0.3048; to convert feet to meters, multiply by 0.3048.
How many pounds are in one kilogram?
One kilogram equals approximately 2.20462 pounds, because one pound is defined as exactly 0.45359237 kilograms. To convert kilograms to pounds, divide by 0.45359237; to convert pounds to kilograms, multiply by 0.45359237.
Why can temperature not be converted with a simple multiplication factor?
Length and weight scales share the same zero point, so they convert with a single multiplication factor. Celsius and Fahrenheit have different zero points, so temperature conversion needs both a multiplication and an addition or subtraction, for example Fahrenheit equals Celsius times 9 divided by 5 plus 32.
Why is a Kelvin value below zero rejected?
Kelvin starts at absolute zero, the lowest temperature physically possible, which equals minus 273.15 degrees Celsius. A negative Kelvin value cannot exist, so this converter shows an error instead of a meaningless result.
Is my conversion data saved or sent anywhere?
No. OmniTools runs entirely in your browser. Your values and conversions are never sent to a server, stored, or shared with anyone.
