How to Use the GPA Calculator

Your Grade Point Average, or GPA, is a single number that summarises how well you have done across all your courses. Colleges, scholarship committees, and some employers use it as a quick measure of academic performance. This calculator works it out for you: add one row per course, choose the letter grade you earned, enter the credit hours, and press Calculate GPA. Your GPA, total credits, course count, and total quality points appear instantly.

  1. Start with the example rowsThe calculator opens with three example courses. Change their grades and credit hours to match your own, or remove them and start fresh.
  2. Add a row for every coursePress Add Course for each additional subject. Include every graded course from the semester or period you are measuring, and leave out pass/fail courses unless your school counts them.
  3. Enter credit hours honestlyCredit hours reflect how much weight a course carries. A 4-credit laboratory science affects your GPA more than a 1-credit seminar, so copying the credits from your transcript matters.
  4. Calculate and read the resultYour GPA appears on the 4.0 scale, together with a plain-language description of what that range means, so you can see at a glance where you stand.

The Standard 4.0 Grade Scale

Most schools in the United States and many international programmes use the 4.0 scale below. Each letter grade is worth a fixed number of grade points, and plus and minus grades sit between the whole letters. An A is worth 4.0, an A-minus 3.7, a B-plus 3.3, and so on down to an F, which is worth zero. Some schools use slightly different values, for example an A-plus at 4.3 or no minus grades at all, so check your own institution's scale if your result needs to match an official transcript exactly. This calculator uses the widely taught standard scale, which is the right default for planning and estimates.

The GPA formula is a weighted average, not a simple average of grades. Each course contributes its grade points multiplied by its credit hours, and those products are called quality points. Add up all the quality points and divide by the total credit hours. For example, an A (4.0) in a 4-credit course, a B (3.0) in a 3-credit course, and a C (2.0) in a 3-credit course give quality points of 16, 9, and 6, a total of 31, divided by 10 credits, which is a GPA of 3.10. Notice that the 4-credit A pulls the average up more than the 3-credit C pulls it down, which is exactly why weighting by credits is fairer than averaging letters alone.

GPA = total quality points / total credit hours
Quality points for one course = grade points x credit hours
Worked example: (4.0 x 4) + (3.0 x 3) + (2.0 x 3) = 16 + 9 + 6 = 31 quality points. 31 / 10 credits = 3.10 GPA.

Weighted vs. Unweighted GPA, and How to Raise Yours

An unweighted GPA caps every course at 4.0, no matter how hard it is. A weighted GPA gives extra points for honours, Advanced Placement, or International Baccalaureate courses, sometimes up to 5.0, to reward students who take harder classes. This calculator computes an unweighted GPA on the standard 4.0 scale, which is the figure most colleges ask for first; if your school reports a weighted GPA, treat this result as the unweighted baseline and check how your school adds its bonus points.

1. Focus on high-credit courses

Because GPA is credit-weighted, improving a 4-credit course moves your GPA twice as much as improving a 2-credit one. Put extra study time where the credits are.

2. Think in semesters, not single grades

One low grade hurts less as you accumulate credits. Run the calculator with your current GPA plus planned future grades to see what you need next semester to reach your target.

3. Retakes and grade replacement

Some schools replace a failed grade when you retake a course, while others average both attempts. Ask your registrar which policy applies before assuming a retake erases the old grade.

A GPA is a useful summary, but it is not the whole story of a student. Admissions officers also read essays, recommendations, and the difficulty of your courses, and a rising trend across semesters often impresses more than a flat average. Use this calculator to plan and to check your progress, and keep the bigger picture in mind. All calculations run privately in your browser, so your grades are never sent to a server or stored anywhere.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is a GPA?
A Grade Point Average (GPA) is a number, usually on a 0 to 4.0 scale, that summarises your grades across courses. Each letter grade is worth a set number of points, and your GPA is the credit-weighted average of those points: total quality points divided by total credit hours.
What are quality points?
Quality points measure how much a single course contributes to your GPA. They equal the grade points for the letter grade multiplied by the course's credit hours. An A (4.0) in a 3-credit course earns 12 quality points, while the same A in a 1-credit course earns only 4.
What is a good GPA?
It depends on your goals, but generally a GPA of 3.5 or above is considered strong, 3.0 to 3.49 is good, and 2.0 is usually the minimum to stay in good academic standing. Competitive scholarships and graduate programmes often look for 3.5 or higher, alongside essays and recommendations.
Why is my GPA not a simple average of my grades?
Because courses carry different credit hours. A 4-credit course should count more than a 1-credit course, so GPA weights each grade by its credits. Averaging letters without weighting would overstate easy, low-credit courses and understate demanding ones.
Does this calculator use my school's exact scale?
It uses the standard 4.0 scale: A 4.0, A- 3.7, B+ 3.3, B 3.0, B- 2.7, C+ 2.3, C 2.0, C- 1.7, D 1.0, F 0.0. Some schools differ slightly, for example by offering A+ or no minus grades, so check your institution's scale for an official figure.
Is my grade data stored anywhere?
No. The calculator runs entirely in your browser. Your grades and credit hours are never sent to a server or stored.
