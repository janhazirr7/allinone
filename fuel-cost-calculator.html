How to Use the Fuel Cost Calculator

A road trip, a daily commute, or a delivery route all come down to the same question: how much will the fuel actually cost? Guessing usually means an unpleasant surprise at the pump. This calculator gives you the exact answer in seconds, from three numbers you already know or can easily find: how far you are going, how much fuel your vehicle uses, and what fuel costs right now.

  1. Choose your unit systemPick Metric if you measure distance in kilometres and consumption in litres per 100 km, or Imperial if you use miles and miles per gallon (MPG). The labels and units change automatically.
  2. Enter the trip distanceType the total distance. For a round trip, add both legs together. For example, a 250 km drive each way becomes 500 km.
  3. Enter your fuel consumptionUse your car's average figure, usually shown on the dashboard or in the owner's manual. A typical small petrol car uses around 6 to 8 litres per 100 km, roughly 30 to 40 MPG.
  4. Enter the fuel price and calculateType the current price per litre or per gallon, then press Calculate Cost. You will see the total trip cost, the exact amount of fuel needed, and the cost per kilometre or mile.

The Fuel Cost Formula, Explained Simply

The maths behind the calculator is straightforward, and understanding it helps you trust the result and spot mistakes in your inputs. In the metric system, fuel needed is the distance divided by 100, multiplied by the consumption figure. A 500 km trip in a car that uses 7 litres per 100 km needs 500 divided by 100, which is 5, times 7, which is 35 litres. Multiply those 35 litres by the pump price, say $1.80 per litre, and the trip costs $63.00.

Fuel needed (litres) = distance (km) x consumption (L/100 km) / 100
Total cost = fuel needed x price per litre
Imperial version: fuel needed (gallons) = distance (miles) / MPG, then total cost = gallons x price per gallon. Example: 300 miles at 30 MPG needs 10 gallons; at $3.50 per gallon that is $35.00.

Converting between the two systems is a common stumbling block. Litres per 100 km and MPG are inversely related: a higher MPG number means a more efficient car, while a lower L/100 km number means the same thing. The conversion constant is 235.2145833. To turn MPG into L/100 km, divide 235.2145833 by the MPG figure, so 30 MPG is about 7.84 L/100 km. To go the other way, divide 235.2145833 by the L/100 km figure, so 7.5 L/100 km is about 31.4 MPG. If your car shows one unit and a fuel station or rental quote uses the other, this conversion keeps your estimate honest.

Why Your Real Fuel Use May Differ

Dashboard consumption figures are averages measured under test conditions, and real driving rarely matches them exactly. City driving with constant stopping and starting uses far more fuel than steady motorway cruising. Cold starts, air conditioning, roof boxes, heavy loads, under-inflated tyres, and aggressive acceleration all push consumption up, sometimes by 10 to 30 percent. That is why it is wise to add a small buffer to the calculator's result when budgeting a long trip, especially in winter or on mountain roads.

1. City vs. motorway

A car rated at 6 L/100 km on the motorway may use 9 L/100 km in city traffic. If your trip mixes both, use a blended average closer to your real driving, or calculate each leg separately and add the results.

2. Check your real average

Fill the tank, reset the trip meter, drive normally, then refill and divide litres used by kilometres driven. That measured figure beats any brochure number and makes future estimates far more accurate.

3. Save fuel, save money

Gentle acceleration, steady speeds, correct tyre pressure, and removing a roof box when you do not need it can cut consumption noticeably. On a 500 km trip, dropping from 8 to 7 L/100 km saves 5 litres, which is real money on every journey.

The calculator is also handy beyond personal trips. Delivery drivers can price jobs accurately, families can split trip costs fairly among passengers, and anyone comparing two cars can see what the efficiency difference is worth in cash over a year. Multiply your weekly distance by 52, run it through the calculator, and the annual fuel bill difference between a thirsty car and an efficient one becomes concrete rather than abstract. All calculations happen privately in your browser, with no data sent anywhere, so you can plan any trip with confidence.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

How do I calculate fuel cost for a trip?
Multiply your trip distance by your vehicle's consumption to find the fuel needed, then multiply that by the fuel price. In metric units: fuel needed in litres equals distance in km times L/100 km divided by 100. In imperial units: fuel needed in gallons equals distance in miles divided by MPG. This calculator does both steps for you.
How do I convert MPG to L/100 km?
Divide 235.2145833 by the MPG figure. For example, 30 MPG equals about 7.84 L/100 km. To convert the other way, divide 235.2145833 by the L/100 km figure, so 7.5 L/100 km equals about 31.4 MPG. Remember the relationship is inverse: higher MPG and lower L/100 km both mean better efficiency.
Where do I find my car's fuel consumption?
Check the dashboard trip computer, which usually shows an average figure, or your owner's manual. For the most accurate number, measure it yourself: fill the tank, reset the trip meter, drive normally, refill, and divide the litres used by the kilometres driven, then multiply by 100.
Why is my real fuel cost higher than the estimate?
Official consumption figures are measured under test conditions. City traffic, cold starts, air conditioning, heavy loads, roof boxes, low tyre pressure, and fast acceleration can all raise real consumption by 10 to 30 percent. Adding a small buffer to your estimate is wise for long trips.
Does the calculator store my trip data?
No. Every calculation runs entirely in your browser. No distance, consumption, or price you enter is sent to a server or stored anywhere.
